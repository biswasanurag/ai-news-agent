# AI Briefing: Saturday, November 21, 2026

## Overview
Anthropic had a big week. A mathematician there claims Claude helped him prove the Riemann hypothesis, the company went public, and METR published new capability measurements.

## Top stories

**Anthropic mathematician claims Claude helped prove the Riemann hypothesis**
Levent Alpöge says he used Claude to find a Lean-formalized proof of the Riemann hypothesis. Mathematicians including MIT's Andrew Sutherland say they have confirmed that the Lean proof compiles. Experts have not yet worked through the argument itself, and no human-readable explanation exists. A separate, purely human proof attempt by Alessandro Masullo was posted too. It has not been formally checked.

Why it matters: If the proof holds up, it would be a landmark for AI-assisted formal mathematics, and it also shows how Lean can verify AI-generated work.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1010051/anthropic-riemann-hypothesis-millennium-prize-problem)

**Anthropic goes public on Nasdaq amid debate over safety**
Anthropic is reportedly the first pure AI lab to go public. It priced at $83 a share and climbed as high as $111 on its first day. The Verge discusses whether its safety-first mission can coexist with shareholder pressure and compute bills of roughly $900 billion over the coming years. The discussion also covers its unusual governance structure.

Why it matters: Public-market pressure could shape how the maker of Claude balances safety commitments against growth.
[The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/1038701/anthropic-ipo-ai-safety-openai-nvidia-structure)

**METR: AI capabilities still improving exponentially, but judgment lags**
METR updated its time-horizon plot with longer and more open-ended tasks. Capabilities continue to improve exponentially on some measures. Models still fall short on judgment-heavy tasks. For example, none of the AI-designed evaluations were useful to METR staff, and models underperformed humans at scoring philosophical arguments.

Why it matters: Progress on verifiable tasks like coding may not carry over to fuzzier work, which limits how far agents can automate research.
[MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/20/1149399/the-most-misunderstood-graph-in-ai-just-got-a-major-update/)
